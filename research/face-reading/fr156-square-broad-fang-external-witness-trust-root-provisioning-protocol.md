# FR156 — Square-Broad Fang External Witness Trust-Root Provisioning Protocol

## Status

FR156 freezes the requirements for a future **externally governed witness trust root** and semantic witness-trust verification boundary.

It does not provision a root, pin a key, authenticate an issuer, verify semantic credential contents, trust a witness, or admit independent sessions.

The protocol exists to prevent a later implementation from turning caller-supplied references, caller-generated keys, digest matches, mathematically valid signatures, self-signed credentials, synthetic credentials, or historical capture labels into unearned external trust.

## Predecessor boundary

FR155 can verify candidate trust-evidence artifact byte identity and bind bounded candidate records to exact FR154 signer/witness coordinates. FR155 explicitly does not establish issuer identity, issuer trust, semantic credential validity, signer-key trust, witness-authority trust, or a governed trust root.

FR156 preserves that boundary. Candidate trust evidence is an input candidate for a future governed verifier; it is not a credential with authority by itself.

## Frozen requirements for future trust-root admission

Before a witness trust root can be admitted, the future admission boundary must require all of the following:

1. **External governance authority identity** — the authority behind the root must be independently verified rather than inferred from a caller-supplied reference.
2. **Trust-root artifact byte identity** — the exact root artifact bytes must match a declared canonical artifact identity.
3. **Semantic trust-root verification** — byte identity alone is insufficient; the artifact must be parsed and semantically verified under a governed policy.
4. **Externally governed key pinning** — the trusted root key must be pinned by governance external to the caller and this research process.
5. **Validity and revocation policy** — a governed policy must define whether the root remains valid and how revocation/status is evaluated.
6. **Signer-chain and semantic trust-evidence verification** — a governed verifier must bind FR155 candidate evidence and FR154 signer coordinates to the externally governed root.

The exact production witness verification algorithm remains unresolved. `pinnedWitnessTrustRootRef` therefore remains null.

## Explicit insufficiency rules

The following are insufficient to establish the trust root or witness trust:

- a caller-supplied trust-root reference;
- a caller-supplied public key;
- a byte-for-byte digest match by itself;
- a mathematically valid signature by itself;
- a self-signed certificate or credential;
- a synthetic certificate or credential generated by tests or this project;
- a claimed issuer reference;
- an opaque policy reference without policy verification;
- FR154 mechanical signature validity;
- FR155 candidate trust evidence;
- an FR155 candidate bundle digest; or
- historical FR146/FR147 capture material.

A self-generated root cannot become “external” merely because it is encoded in a certificate-like structure. A synthetic root can test parser mechanics, but it cannot establish external governance authority.

## Historical evidence non-promotion

Historical FR146 A/B are not prospective independent sessions and are not eligible as a substitute for external trust-root evidence.

FR156 does not alter that fact. Different bytes, different opaque refs, earlier operator-declared session refs, or later signatures over historical references cannot retroactively create independently witnessed prospective capture provenance.

## Privacy boundary

FR156 is a protocol-freeze artifact. It does not accept trust-root artifact bytes at runtime and does not accept raw face images or source-image digests.

It does not persist or return:

- raw face media;
- raw provider responses;
- raw pixel rasters;
- raw aggregate geometry;
- source-image digests;
- trust-root artifact bytes;
- signer public-key PEM;
- detached signature bytes;
- embeddings;
- identity templates;
- exact capture timestamps;
- geolocation; or
- device identifiers.

Future trust-root admission may need transient trust-root artifact bytes for byte-identity and semantic verification, but those bytes are outside FR156 itself and do not authorize any source-face digest handling.

## Synthetic test boundary

Public tests may materialize the FR156 protocol object and verify its fail-closed rules deterministically.

Synthetic tests cannot prove that an external governance authority exists, that a real root is trusted, that an issuer is authentic, that credential semantics are valid, that a signer belongs to a witness authority, or that any two captures are independent sessions.

## Real-world blocker remains

Even after a future external trust root is genuinely provisioned, independent-session admission still also requires genuinely new source-backed prospective capture sessions acquired under the frozen FR152 protocol with independently supportable witness evidence and session separation.

FR156 therefore does not move repeatability, construct validity, capture-quality authority, or traditional-semantic authority.
