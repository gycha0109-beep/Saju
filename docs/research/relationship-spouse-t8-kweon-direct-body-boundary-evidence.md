## What the actual body establishes

### 1. Traditional spouse interpretation remains gender-conditioned in the historical discussion

Printed p.48 discusses the traditional placement of the husband in a female chart under `官星` and explains how that framing arose within a male-centered hierarchical social system.

This historical discussion is not itself a new role-neutral rule. It is important because the thesis explicitly treats parts of inherited kinship interpretation as socially contingent rather than timeless.

### 2. Marriage interpretation uses both Ten Gods and spouse palace

Printed pp.77–78 describe conventional marriage analysis as observing both:

```text
spouse-related 財官 states
+
spouse palace / 日支
```

The thesis explicitly calls the Day Branch (`日支`) the spouse palace.

This confirms the palace semantic surface directly. It still does not tell the engine how to select a spouse/partner meaning independently of sex.

### 3. Same-sex cohabitation family is directly in scope

Printed pp.81–82 discuss changing family structures and explicitly include `동성애 동거 가족` among modern family forms.

The thesis treats such a relationship as capable of functioning as the participants' family even where Korean law does not recognize same-sex marriage.

This is a material modern-family applicability finding. It must not be used to infer any user's sexual orientation, partner sex, or desired family form.

### 4. Traditional 財官-only marriage scope is explicitly described as insufficient

Printed p.83 is the decisive passage for the old role-neutral frontier.

The thesis states that the same-sex marriage form has already moved beyond the range traditionally judged through Wealth/Officer-centered Ten-God interpretation and argues that non-traditional marriage requires an expanded definition of Yukchin.

It then says that because marriage logic may be derived not only from Ten Gods but also from palace position, attention naturally turns to the Day Branch as spouse palace.

This is stronger than the earlier abstract-only signal.

However, it is still an **alternative interpretive direction**, not a complete operational mapping.

The reviewed body does not provide one rule of the form:

```text
for every native and partner,
regardless of native sex and partner sex,
select spouse/partner meaning from canonical natal inputs by X
```

No such selector or complete input contract appears in the reviewed target pages or conclusion.

### 5. The concrete same-sex chart example remains gender-conditioned and is not admissible product authority

The same p.83 passage cites an external teaching source as an example of recently proposed same-sex chart interpretation.

That cited rule:

- splits the logic into male-chart and female-chart branches;
- evaluates Ten-God patterns in the marriage palace differently by those branches; and
- attempts to infer homosexual tendency from those chart patterns.

This is decisive for the product boundary.

```text
cited concrete example             = EXISTS
role-neutral                       = NO
gender-conditioned                 = YES
sexual-orientation inference       = YES
product-admissible identity rule   = NO
```

The repository must not use a Saju chart to infer sexual orientation or partner gender, nor may it silently neutralize the cited male/female branches.

The cited example is also not the thesis author publishing a newly validated, complete partner-neutral selector; it is evidence that the research field is searching for solutions.

### 6. The conclusion remains a reinterpretation agenda, not an executable spouse contract

Printed pp.84–85 conclude that traditional concepts often fail to fit modern life and that Ten-God meanings can be reinterpreted and expanded according to changing family, marriage, and social relations.

The conclusion emphasizes continuing reinterpretation and the need for new solutions.

It does **not** crystallize the same-sex-family discussion into a governed spouse-specific algorithm or a complete role-neutral natal input contract.
