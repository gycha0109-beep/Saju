## 3. What the actual body establishes

### 3.1 The classical method is already more complex than `husband = Officer`

Printed p.313 reproduces the `夫星` selection logic attributed to `滴天髓闡微` and explains cases where, if Officer is unusable, another Ten-God class is treated as `夫` through the balancing/Yongshin logic.

The paper also immediately clarifies a critical historical-method boundary: those substitutions ultimately assist the use of Officer in the classical framework. They are not automatically a modern role-neutral spouse selector.

### 3.2 The author explicitly reopens spouse representation under modern relationships

Printed pp.325-326 move from historical explanation to the author’s modern analysis.

The source explicitly asks what husband may represent if a modern woman uses Officer for profession, whether household itself should be treated as another Officer-like organization, and whether the same reasoning should apply to a man responsible for a household.

It then gives concrete relationship-role examples:

```text
woman is the household economic center
→ husband may be represented through Output or Wealth-like role semantics

equal shared economic/household burden
→ Peer may be the partner relation
```

These are source-authored examples, not a repository synthesis.

### 3.3 Worked cases do not support a one-symbol deterministic rule

Printed pp.327-331 compare several modern female cases with the classical husband-star rules.

The paper records, among other things:

- women with no Officer or excessive Officer may marry and maintain marriages;
- women with strong natal Officer or Officer luck may remain unmarried;
- some cases use a different Yongshin as `夫`;
- one worked case describes an equal-age/equal-status spouse through Peer/Yongshin logic;
- another uses the actual Day-Branch Peer as `夫`;
- marriage timing may still correlate with Officer luck even when the represented spouse relation is not reduced to Officer.

This materially rejects a simple `Officer present => husband outcome` mapping.

### 3.4 Conclusion explicitly extends the proposal to male charts

Printed pp.332-334 are decisive.

The source states that the classical `husband = Officer, wife = Wealth` view is not wholly useless, but that modern social conditions and the studied cases mean a woman’s husband need not be limited to Officer.

The author then explicitly proposes:

- spouse may be used through Ten Gods other than Officer;
- Peer can be a genuine equal companion under equal partnership;
- in a household centered economically on the woman, husband may be represented through Output or Wealth;
- if such a relation also aligns with the native’s Yongshin, the author treats that as especially favorable;
- **the same approach can also be applied to male charts**;
- a cited Xu Le-wu passage is used to reinforce the view that female and male chart usage should not be fundamentally different;
- the paper further states that a man may likewise treat spouse and children as his Yongshin/Heesin.

This is the strongest source-authored modern sex-neutral spouse-remapping proposal found in this track so far.

## 4. Why this still does not close `EXPLICIT_ROLE_NEUTRAL_NATAL_MAPPING`

The paper’s strongest positive result is not a pure-natal selector.

Its modern examples choose or motivate spouse Ten-God semantics through facts such as:

```text
who is the economic center of the household
whether the couple wants equal partnership
how economy and housework are divided
how roles change during the relationship
what kind of spouse/family the native wants
the native's intentional use of chart symbols
Yongshin / Heesin
```

The conclusion itself describes destiny/Yongshin as involving the native’s will and how the subject uses it.

Therefore:

```text
raw natal chart
→ one deterministic partner-sex-independent spouse Ten-God
```

is **not** published by this source.

Instead the method is closer to:

```text
natal structure
+ Yongshin/Heesin semantics
+ desired or lived household / partner role
+ subject intention / use
→ contextual spouse representation
```

That is a real method, but it is not the repository’s missing pure-natal executable spouse selector.

The source also does not publish a single partner-sex-independent rule covering every possible partner identity. It extends the interpretive logic from women to men, but the reviewed body does not define a complete selector contract for partner sex, nonbinary identity, or same-sex partnership. Those must not be inferred.
