## Direct-body findings

### Printed p.633 / physical PDF 3

The article explicitly refuses to treat every sex distinction in traditional Mingli at the same analytical level. It distinguishes:

- internal calculation and kinship-assignment rules; from
- normative value judgments that moralize female strength, sexuality, chastity, husband harm, and similar categories.

The paper states that its analytical focus is the second layer.

This distinction is decisive for the frozen admission question. A reform of gendered moral evaluation is not automatically a reform of the spouse-selection rule.

### Printed p.636 / physical PDF 6

The article explains the technical convention it is analyzing:

```text
female chart → Officer / 官星 → mainly husband
male chart   → Wealth / 財星 → wife and concubine
```

It also notes that Daewoon calculation is differentiated by sex. The article therefore does not itself present a fully sex-independent technical calculation layer at this point.

### Printed p.649 / physical PDF 19

The article repeats the technical-versus-normative distinction in spouse-specific terms. Assigning Officer as husband is treated as a technical kinship rule; labeling a person immoral, disobedient, promiscuous, or responsible for a husband's harm is treated as a separate normative judgment.

This page prevents an inference that rejecting the latter automatically abolishes or replaces the former.

### Printed p.657 / physical PDF 27

The paper reports that traditional material itself contains criticism and revision pressure. It also reports a line of interpretation in which a woman's husband is not fixed only to Officer and may be considered through broader structure/useful-god reasoning.

That is relevant historical flexibility, but this article does not turn that discussion into a new complete spouse-specific operational selector of its own. It does not specify one role-neutral algorithm that is independent of native sex and partner sex and executable from natal facts alone.
